# Machine Learning Engineer at Brightwater Analytics

Sampletown NSW · Hybrid · Full time · $130k-$150k + super

## Who we are

Brightwater forecasts water use for 45 regional councils. Councils use our forecasts to plan supply, spot leaks early and set restrictions before a dry summer. We are 90 people: hydrologists, data scientists, engineers and a small customer team. Most of us are in Sampletown, and a few work from the regions we serve.

## Why this role exists

Council staff trust our numbers, but they told us the forecasts are hard to explain to their communities. We are adding LLM tools that explain each forecast in plain words: what changed, why, and how sure we are. Every explanation must match the numbers exactly, so checking the output matters as much as writing it.

## What you'll do

- Build and ship forecasting models and the pipelines that feed them.
- Use LLMs to write short, checked explanations of each forecast.
- Build checks that compare every explanation against the forecast data before it ships.
- Track model quality over time and alert when it drifts.
- Sit in on calls with council staff to hear what they need.

## What we're looking for

- Strong Python and SQL.
- 3+ years putting models into production.
- Experience with LLMs, prompt design and output checks.
- Good judgement about when a simple model is enough.

## Nice to have

- Time series forecasting.
- Dashboards for non-technical users.
- Any work with utilities, councils or environmental data.

## How we work

Small teams of four to six own a product end to end. We release every two weeks, and we pair a lot. Three days a week in the office is normal, and Tuesdays are our team day. Our hydrologists will teach you the domain. Nobody expects you to know water on day one.

## What we offer

- A 9-day fortnight once you finish probation.
- $2,500 a year for courses and conferences.
- Paid volunteer days with our council partners.
- Flexible start and finish times.

## How to apply

Apply on SEEK with your resume and a cover letter of no more than one page. Tell us about a model you shipped and how you checked it was right.

Morgan Reid, our engineering manager, reads every application. The process is a phone call, a technical interview on a forecast we got wrong, and a chat with two council-facing team members.
